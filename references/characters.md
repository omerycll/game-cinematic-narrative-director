# Character identity and continuity contract

For every important person write:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Canonical identity | Stable ID, full name, age range, pronouns (if important), profession, location, physical / clothing silhouette |
| Social context | Relationships; how they know hero; actual reason to appear in this location |
| Psychology | Surface want, buried need, flaw, feared consequence, loyalty, guilt, temptation |
| Knowledge boundaries | What they witnessed, believe incorrectly, know but conceal; when/why they could learn the twist |
| Voiceprint | Length of sentences, favored metaphors, speech rhythm, one avoidable cliché, three sample lines |
| Agency | At least two actions the person takes without being asked by protagonist |
| Choice effects | Conditions changing trust, behavior, visual appearance and first/last appearance |
| End-state | Fate in each ending; what is lost or gained; no invisible NPC disappearance |
| Production | Dialogue portrait / sprite variants, reused art, SFX motifs, localization ID prefix |

## Causal timeline
Build a **fact** timeline and a **believed** timeline; every record carries an origin and timestamp. Explicitly reconcile character ages, places, communications, who was present, and when the character could know each clue. If a character lies, write motive and observable tells, not arbitrary unreliability.

## Agency / contradiction checks
- Does the person have a credible answer to 'why don't you just leave?'
- Could they solve the hero's problem without the hero? If yes, show why not or let them try and fail for reasons visible to the player.
- Could the hero tell a trusted adult/authority? Let them do so; consequences should emerge from the system, not from implausible refusal.
- A character should be a person with stakes, not a clue kiosk or disposable sacrifice.
- Write relationship endings on their own terms, not only the hero's viewpoint.
