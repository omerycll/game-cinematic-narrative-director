# Psychological horror and mystery design

## Fear dynamics
Make dread emerge from evidence and responsibility, in waves:
ordinary warmth -> small falsifiable anomaly -> plausible repair -> repeat anomaly -> personal knowledge -> boundary violation -> consequence of player's action -> apparent refuge -> recontextualization -> moral cost -> final residue. Insert quiet, human scenes after heavy beats.

## Four mystery ledgers
1. **Truth:** literal cause, scope, cost, impossibility boundaries.
2. **Witness:** which characters saw which fact, and when.
3. **Player:** mandatory and optional clues, false interpretations, growing hypotheses.
4. **Machine:** which phenomena the threat can/cannot create; how it communicates and why it needs human action.

## Fair-reveal audit
Each major twist must have >=2 prior witnessable breadcrumbs, one plausible false inference, and an after-the-fact explanation that preserves every shown observation. Give hints about mechanisms via playable tests, not expository lectures. Never let a character know a secret for which there is no acquisition path.

## Suspense under agency
Player actions should be risky but logically comprehensible. The player's desire to inspect an unfamiliar object, investigate a recurring anomaly or approach a dangerous location must lead to novel knowledge or consequence; avoid 'you lose because you tapped it' traps. Allow the hero to ask obvious safety questions and call for help. Audio-only jumpscares must have an accessible visual equivalent.

## Fear test
If a line threatens 'the whole world,' replace abstract scale with a personal, verifiable consequence in the current room. If the force appears all-powerful, introduce a limitation and a fair chance to exploit it. If characters act stupid only to advance the plot, rewrite the obstacle.

## Content boundaries
Choose intended rating and mark warnings. Prioritize isolation, altered familiarity, memory discrepancies and moral discomfort over graphic gore or exploitative depiction of vulnerable people. Respect player's ability to pause and read.
