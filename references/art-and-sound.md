# Game cinematography, sound and art bible

## Required visual system
Art thesis, silhouette grammar, environmental motifs, orientation-appropriate composition and interaction zones, foreground/midground/background split, interactive object legibility, state-variant list, value-range contrast, emotional color script, character turnarounds, sprite counts and reusable lighting overlays.

## Per-scene art ticket
`ART_ID | room geometry revision | layer list | target resolution and device safe area | focal path | lighting source | camera distance | sprite/rig | 3 key animation loops | sound anchor | click coordinate zones | narrative meaning | version + budget tier`

## Cinematic rules
Use camera restrictions as a creative strength. Wide shot for isolation, close insert for control, hold after consequence, no camera motion for empty shock. Make recurring props shift meaning without altering their geometry. Color grading cannot hide interactables. Define 2-3 frames of environmental microanimation before requesting elaborate character rigs.

## Audio
Specify sound *and silence*, whether music is diegetic, stereo positioning only if helpful, scene-to-scene motifs and why a cue happens. Provide captions, waveform/spectrum visual equivalents and adjustable SFX/music/voice. Dramatic sound timing must not be a timing-only mandatory puzzle.

## Budget
Count **base rooms**, **state variants**, **hotspots**, **hero rigs**, **named NPC portrait sets**, **inserts**, **sound cues** and **UI** independently. Writing 30 scenes in 12 reused rooms is not 30 unique paintings; verify visual changes are enough to communicate progress.

## AI asset consistency
Produce a canonical scene reference sheet for camera position and room layout; each subsequent image prompt must preserve door/window coordinates, perspective, furniture silhouette and light source. Do not claim a generated concept image is in-engine gameplay or editable layered production art.
